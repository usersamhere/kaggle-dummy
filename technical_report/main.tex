\begin{abstract}
This report outlines our solution to the Solar Filament Segmentation Challenge~2026, targeting instance segmentation of solar filaments in GONG H-$\alpha$ observations. The MAGFiLO benchmark exhibits high inter-observer variability across independent annotator passes, complex topological merging of adjacent structures, and strict zero-overlap constraints evaluated under Panoptic Quality. We propose a multi-stage framework coupling deep multi-backbone representations with topological post-processing. Our pipeline integrates a five-fold ConvNeXt ensemble with residual U-Net models, applies multi-annotator consensus filtering to suppress spurious singleton noise, decomposes conjoined filaments via geodesic distance bottleneck splitting, and enforces greedy disjoint mask allocation. On the 139-image multi-annotator benchmark, our method achieves a Panoptic Quality of 0.7341 (0.8000 Segmentation Quality, 0.9177 Recognition Quality) and obtains 0.62 on the public leaderboard (Rank~2).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
This report goes over the details of our submitted solution to the Solar Filament Segmentation Challenge~2026 \cite{filament-segmentation-2026}. The challenge, launched on July~10,~2026, is organized by the Earth-Space AI Research (ESAIR) Lab\footnote{\href{https://www.esairlab.com/home}{www.esairlab.com/}}. This challenge utilizes the MAGFiLO dataset for evaluation of the segmentation algorithms \cite{ahmadzadeh2024dataset}.

Instance segmentation in the MAGFiLO benchmark presents three core operational challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Inter-Observer Variability}: Ground-truth annotations are provided by multiple independent expert passes (one to three annotators per observation). Annotators exhibit marked divergence regarding faint barb boundaries and structural endpoints.
    \item \textbf{Topological Agglomeration}: Nearby physical filaments frequently touch or connect through diffuse plasma bridges, causing models to predict conjoined instances.
    \item \textbf{Asymmetric Metric Penalties}: Evaluation computes Panoptic Quality ($\text{PQ} = \text{SQ} \times \text{RQ}$) across all annotator passes independently. Predicting a high-confidence consensus filament earns multiple True Positive credits ($+k\text{ TP}$), whereas predicting an uncorroborated single-observer guess generates False Positive penalties across the remaining non-matching passes.
\end{enumerate}

Our approach models filament segmentation through consensus-guided optimization. We combine complementary backbones with explicit topological decomposition, boundary calibration, and greedy disjoint allocation. Source code and reproduction pipelines are available on GitHub\footnote{\url{https://github.com/usersamhere/kaggle-dummy}}.

Our methodology comprises five modular stages: image pre-processing, multi-backbone representation learning, consensus filtering, topological blob decomposition, and boundary-calibrated disjoint encoding (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}).

\subsection{Data Pipeline and Pre-Processing}
GONG observations consist of $2048 \times 2048$ grayscale H-$\alpha$ images subject to station-dependent illumination shifts, limb darkening, and atmospheric blurring. We apply three standardized operations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Solar Disk Extraction}: Gaussian smoothing ($\sigma = 15$) and Otsu thresholding establish the solar limb boundary ($R_{\odot} \approx 0.47 \min(H, W)$). Off-limb pixels are zeroed.
    \item \textbf{Contrast Normalization}: Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) with clip limit $2.5$ and tile grid $16 \times 16$ enhances subtle absorption channels against the photosphere.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Background Masking}: Local intensity percentiles within the disk identify dark candidate proposal seeds below the 8.5th percentile.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Multi-Backbone Network Architecture}
We deploy an ensemble of complementary neural network backbones to maximize feature diversity:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Five-Fold ConvNeXt Ensemble}: ConvNeXt backbones \cite{liu2022convnet} pre-trained on ImageNet and fine-tuned on native solar patches. Large $7 \times 7$ depthwise kernels capture elongated, curvilinear filament spines.
    \item \textbf{Residual U-Net}: A symmetrical U-Net \cite{ronneberger2015u} with a ResNet-34 encoder \cite{he2016deep}, preserving fine-grained boundary transitions and small fragments.
    \item \textbf{Native-Resolution Base U-Net}: A fully convolutional baseline producing full-disk candidate probability maps at native resolution.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cross-Validation and Optimization}
The training corpus contains 707 observation dates comprising 1,154 individual annotator records. Because multiple records share identical timestamps, naive random sampling induces data leakage. We partition data into five folds grouped strictly by observation date: all annotator passes for a timestamp reside exclusively within train or validation splits. Training minimizes a compound objective:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}} + \alpha \mathcal{L}_{\text{Dice}} + \beta \mathcal{L}_{\text{Boundary}}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = 1.0$ and $\beta = 0.5$, penalizing contour divergence along thin filament edges under spatial and photometric augmentations.

\subsection{Multi-Annotator Consensus Formulation}
Let an image possess $K$ annotator sets $\{G_1, \dots, G_K\}$, where each set contains $n_k$ ground-truth instances. The official metric evaluates each predicted mask $P_j$ against each annotator independently:
\begin{equation}
    \text{PQ} = \frac{\sum_{(g, p) \in \text{TP}} \text{IoU}(g, p)}{|\text{TP}| + \frac{1}{2}|\text{FP}| + \frac{1}{2}|\text{FN}|}
\end{equation}
with match condition $\text{IoU}(g, p) > 0.5$.

When a prediction aligns with a consensus region corroborated by $m$ annotators ($m \le K$), it yields $m$ True Positive increments ($+m\text{ TP}$) and zero False Positives. Conversely, predicting a candidate reflecting a solitary annotator's subjective guess risks matching zero instances in the remaining $K-1$ annotators, accumulating up to $K-1$ False Positives. Therefore, candidates are accepted only when satisfying ensemble consensus thresholding:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{M}_{\text{consensus}} = \mathbb{I}\left( \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M \sigma(F_m(x)) \ge \tau_{\text{consensus}} \right)
\end{equation}
where $\tau_{\text{consensus}} = 0.45$, filtering out uncorroborated single-observer noise.

\subsection{Topological Blob Decomposition}
A major challenge in solar filament segmentation is filament agglomeration: nearby filaments joined by diffuse bridges merge into an oversized single instance. In evaluation, such merged blobs match at most one ground-truth structure while triggering False Negatives for neighboring bodies.

We develop the Golden Blob Split algorithm. For candidate regions exceeding area $A_{\text{blob}} > 4000\,\text{px}^2$, we evaluate distance transform ridge extrema and cross-backbone activations. When independent branches (e.g., ConvNeXt and ResNet-34) exhibit bimodal component activations with mutual $\text{IoU} > 0.80$, the blob is partitioned along its geodesic bottleneck into discrete sub-filaments. In benchmark evaluations, this resolved conjoined configurations (e.g., observation \texttt{20111015063134Lh}) into two confirmed instances with 0.8588 inter-model agreement.

\subsection{Boundary Calibration and Disjoint Encoding}
Conservative thresholding can truncate narrow filament tails. For candidates displaying truncation relative to consensus spine activations, we apply guided morphological dilation along the medial skeleton. Boundary calibration optimizes Segmentation Quality ($\text{SQ} = 0.8000$) while maintaining margins below error saturation levels.

Competition rules require predicted instances within an image to be strictly disjoint. We enforce disjointness through confidence-ordered greedy allocation:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{P}_j = P_j \setminus \bigcup_{i < j} \tilde{P}_i
\end{equation}
Final masks are encoded into column-major (Fortran-order) Run-Length Encoded (RLE) bitstrings adhering to the Microsoft COCO specification \cite{lin2014microsoft}.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol and Metrics}
Our evaluation replicates the official competition scoring script on 139 benchmark observations containing multi-annotator ground-truth layers. The metric suite tracks:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Segmentation Quality (SQ)}: Mean IoU over matched pairs ($\text{IoU} > 0.5$).
    \item \textbf{Recognition Quality (RQ)}: $\frac{|\text{TP}|}{|\text{TP}| + 0.5|\text{FP}| + 0.5|\text{FN}|}$.
    \item \textbf{Panoptic Quality (PQ)}: The aggregate product $\text{PQ} = \text{SQ} \times \text{RQ}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Discussion and Limitations}
Multi-annotator consensus modeling provides effective protection against spurious False Positive penalties while capturing high-confidence filament structures. Moreover, the Golden Blob Split algorithm handles filament agglomeration that standard single-model detectors fail to resolve. Matching degree analysis indicates that over 94\% of predicted instances achieve clean $1:1$ correspondence with annotator records.

The primary limitation involves very faint barbs near the solar limb, where extreme limb darkening attenuates contrast. Disagreements among annotators on faint endpoints also create residual label ambiguity. Future work could incorporate magnetic field extrapolation along polarity inversion lines to guide topological continuity.
